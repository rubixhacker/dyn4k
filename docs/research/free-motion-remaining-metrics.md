## Source facts and implications

1. Integration adds force/gravity contributions to velocity and applies damping, then translates by `v*dt` and rotates about the center by `omega*dt`, subject to configured motion caps. This fixture is below the caps and has no force/damping contributions. Accumulation resets force/torque before processing their lists. These facts support numerical-zero applied/queued force and torque checks for this unchanged-input fixture, not a general zero-error tolerance. [AbstractPhysicsBody accumulation/integration](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/dynamics/AbstractPhysicsBody.java#L685-L836)
2. Before each step the world copies the current transform into the previous transform. Exact equality with the preceding recorded transform is therefore a structural check when callbacks do not mutate the body. Within each capture, matrix entries and translation getters expose the same underlying components. [World step](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/world/AbstractPhysicsWorld.java#L1262-L1275), [Transform getters](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/geometry/Transform.java#L471-L606)
3. Transform rotation multiplies stored sine/cosine components by a new rotation and clamps each to `[-1,1]`. Its angle getter uses `acos(cost)` with the sign of `sint`; it does not independently integrate an angle. This differs from `atan2(sint,cost)` when the pair drifts off the unit circle. Keep the public getter and component metrics separate. [Transform rotation](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/geometry/Transform.java#L136-L159), [angle extraction](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/geometry/Transform.java#L532-L537)
4. The capture's `rotationIncrementSum` adds `atan2(sin(a[n]-a[n-1]),cos(a[n]-a[n-1]))`, using that angle getter. Its step-bound flag says configured maximum rotation is below pi; it is not an independently measured cumulative angle. Here the unchanged segment and small motion make unwrapping meaningful. Its error can share the getter's error. [Capture state](https://github.com/rubixhacker/dyn4k/blob/0995a6bac84de882b0d04b6c3ea18162ca3b4a7c/calibration/src/Capture.java#L148-L162)
5. Existing integration tests exercise short steps, caps and static bodies; transform tests contain particular-angle assertions with `1e-3` deltas. They do not specify 36,000-step component/angle/accumulation budgets. Adopting those deltas here would be a new engineering decision, not preserving a demonstrated long-run guarantee. [Integration tests](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/test/java/org/dyn4j/dynamics/AbstractPhysicsBodyTest.java#L913-L1000), [Transform tests](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/test/java/org/dyn4j/geometry/TransformTest.java#L58-L79)

## Numerical interpretation and unresolved budgets

The analytic rotation oracle uses 75-digit Decimal arithmetic, an 84-decimal-place pi constant, argument reduction and Taylor sine/cosine until both current terms are below `1e-65`. On the reduced interval `[-pi,pi]`, subsequent term magnitudes shrink; truncation and decimal-rounding effects are conservatively below `1e-60` for this bounded calculation. This is an independently implemented analytic reference, not a replay of dyn4j's rotation recurrence. It is not a formally certified interval-arithmetic oracle. Conversion of measured residuals to binary64 adds rounding in their displayed final digits. A fresh stability run at precision 100 / termination `1e-85` retained every one of the 35 metric summaries exactly; among all per-step entries only current unit-circle defect at step 260 and its previous-transform copy at step 261 differed by one binary64 ulp (`3.1554436208840472e-30`). This supports the reported precision without claiming a certified oracle. Choosing exact encoded dt rather than mathematical `1/60` shifts the final analytic angle by only about `4.2e-15` rad; the definition is explicit rather than hidden in a clock sum.

For `acos(c)`, local sensitivity is `1/sqrt(1-c²)` and is unbounded at `c=±1`. A component budget therefore cannot be relabelled as the same angle budget; near an extremum the angular effect scales like the square root of component error. Sign selection and wrapping also matter. The unwrapped sum shares this error, plus wrapped-difference and summation roundoff. A hypothetical ordinary-sum bound `gamma[N]*sum(abs(delta))`, with `gamma[N]=N*u/(1-N*u)` and `u=2^-53`, addresses only the final additions under round-to-nearest binary64 and no exceptional operations; it does not bound the errors of the supplied increments. For 36,000 steps and roughly300 total radians that term alone is about `1.2e-9` rad. It is not a justified overall tolerance.

Python-libm re-evaluation differs slightly from the captured JVM getter/recorder values. JVM baseline evidence cannot establish the accuracy of every Kotlin target's sin/cos/acos/atan2, rounding modes, contraction or compiler operation ordering. No accuracy guarantee for those target operations has been established in this packet. Consequently **no new numeric rotation cap is proposed as justified**. Neither a multiple of the measured maximum nor the short source tests supplies a sufficient basis. A proposed engineering cap would need its own explicitly chosen angular/component resolution and rejection rationale, independent of these observed maxima; a derived bound would need a validated operation/libm error model propagated through clamping, ill-conditioned acos and unwrapping.

Mass/inertia are not recorded dynamically. The circle source computes `m=pi*r²*density`, `I=m*r²/2`; the header agrees with this formula, but repeatedly multiplying the unchanged velocities by those header constants cannot prove that the simulated mass or inertia stayed correct. No mass/inertia or independent conservation acceptance gate is claimed. [Circle mass calculation](https://github.com/dyn4j/dyn4j/blob/058bf6d982a0fb89b54050f929f6ea9dae53b714/src/main/java/org/dyn4j/geometry/Circle.java#L201-L208)

## Consolidated decision and blockers

**Recommended choice:** approve the fixture-specific exact consistency/zero-force checks in the matrix; retain the two already approved numerical families; retain all remaining rotation measurements and momentum/energy values as diagnostics while explicitly keeping the rotational numerical specification open. This approves useful checks without inventing a cross-target budget or omitting a channel.

The remaining blockers are (1) human choice of independently motivated component, wrap-safe public-angle and unwrapped-accumulation budgets, or an approved conditional error model from which to derive them; (2) the corresponding baseline-versus-port comparison rule, which is separate from the analytic metrics above and must not silently reuse their limits; and (3) future executed validation on every required target/runtime. Exact relation checks are within one capture, not zero-error cross-target equality of the trajectory. All other fixtures remain outside this bounded packet. Independent mass/inertia correctness requires existing source/API tests or suitable separately authorized observations; redundant conservation arithmetic here does not discharge it. No additional experiment is required merely to preserve these diagnostics, and none was run.

```sh
python3 docs/research/free-motion-remaining-metrics-audit.py \
  /path/to/L-free-s1.0-o0.0_0.0-hz60-0.jsonl.gz \
  /path/to/dyn4k /path/to/output/measured
```

It rejects incorrect hashes/input identity, non-finite numbers (including exponent overflow), missing/extra state keys and invalid ordering; emits `manifest.json` and every named per-step residual in `samples.jsonl`; and fails if any supplied comparison limit fails. Retrieve the raw member using the earlier report's pinned part013 byte-range shortcut if needed; its exact hash remains mandatory. The auditor records hashes of any `.java` files in the sibling `upstream/` directory; that optional inventory is source provenance, not a prerequisite or numerical validation. For this publication those are fresh immutable-source downloads of `AbstractPhysicsBody`, `AbstractPhysicsWorld`, `AbstractCollisionBody`, `Transform`, `Rotation`, `Circle` and the body/transform test files. No upstream tests were executed; the source tests were inspected. A changed libm can slightly change the derived recomputation diagnostics; it does not authorize a new reference or tolerance.
